# Small-Studio Greenlight Math

> This page is written from the perspective of producers, publishers, and lead designers. It explains the three decision tools Game Radar designs specifically for small teams: the niche-survival scan, same-tier survival math, and the greenlight sandbox (portfolio/single-bet). It contains no genre-specific conclusions and no performance figures.

Big studios play the macro game: head genres, mature tracks, long-term operations. Small teams face an entirely different problem — limited budgets, limited headcount, and possibly no second chance after a failure. Game Radar breaks this into three computable questions:

1. **Where do we stand** — the niche-survival scan
2. **Can we survive there** — same-tier survival math
3. **Should we build, how many, and how much each** — the greenlight sandbox

## 1. The Niche-Survival Scan: Where Do We Stand

Each genre root gets a 0-100 "survival score", combined from four components:

| Component | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Demand heat | Are people discussing and searching this genre |
| Supply gap | How many teams are already building in it — is supply saturated |
| Head-brand breakability | The more scattered the leaders' buzz share (CR1), the wider the entry gap |
| Small-team-won evidence | Are there surviving games in the genre with small review counts — direct evidence that "a small team has won here" |

The total is then discounted by three factors: supply floods (upcoming releases piling up), big-studio siphons (head titles sucking away buzz), and overall market cooling.

**How to use**: the decision weekly serves the survival-score TOP3 directly — not an abstract "genre popularity board", but a board of positions where a small team can stand. The companion same-tier survival math calculator lets you run break-even figures on any single candidate.

## 2. Same-Tier Survival Math: Can We Survive There

Enter three numbers: budget, price, and the benchmark genre. The system computes a break-even chain:

**Budget → break-even units → break-even review count** (converted via the ownership/review ratio) → compared against the review distribution of same-tier benchmarks (titles within 1/3× to 3× of the reference size) → a **CLEAR / TIGHT / BELOW** verdict:

- **CLEAR**: teams of comparable size have survived on similar review counts — you can compute your margin
- **TIGHT**: right on the break-even line, with almost no room for trial and error
- **BELOW**: breaking even would require performance beyond same-tier common sense — proceed with caution

**How to use**: in greenlight meetings, don't debate "I think it can work" — debate "in this BELOW verdict, which assumption do we beat the benchmark teams on". Subjective disagreement gets forced onto concrete assumptions.

## 3. The Greenlight Sandbox: Which Games, How Much Each, When to Start

### Portfolio mode

Enter total budget and planning horizon; get a portfolio recommendation:

- Scored and ranked by scorecard × backtest × forecast, three factors
- UA poison excluded with one veto
- At most 2 projects per genre, staggered 60 days around supply floods
- 80-20 budget allocation (capped at 40% per item)

### Single-bet mode (the small studio's all-in view)

Only one recommendation, with a **GO / WAIT / NO-GO** verdict: the adopted item takes the full 80% pool without splitting, and reserves are used only for rescue — discipline before optimism. Suited to studios that can only run one project at a time.

## 4. Fake-Ad Concept Tests: One More Check Before Greenlight

Is the genre momentum real or inflated? Test it at the lowest possible cost:

1. Register concept creatives (attached to entities/channels/images)
2. AI creative brief: selling-point hooks, fake-trailer directions ×3, channel audiences, CTR baseline rules
3. Backfill impressions/clicks per channel
4. Auto-summarized, color-coded CTR, cross-checked against the signals' virtual backtests

If nobody clicks the concept art, the genre is cold no matter how hot it looks — data precedes investment.

## 5. The Greenlight Lens in the Decision Weekly

The scheduled decision weekly carries a dedicated "small-studio greenlight lens":

- **Survival-score TOP3**: this week's most watchable niche positions
- **Lifecycle candidates**: which lifecycle stage each candidate genre is in (emerging/rising/mature/declining), answering "will demand still be there when we finish"
- **Siphon timing alerts**: when head titles will suck away buzz, so you avoid the head-on window
- **Single-bet discipline**: whether an all-in project still meets its single-bet preconditions

Headed by an AI editor's note (publishing-director persona): who to【launch now】and who to【avoid】this week, a one-sentence scheduling verdict — turning the weekly from an information digest into a scheduling ruling.
